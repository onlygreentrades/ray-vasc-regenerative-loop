# APPRE Future Software and AI Pathway v0.1.1

APPRE is currently released as a public research-data architecture and schema-and-rules framework for repeated measures, confounder annotation, adverse-event capture, missingness handling, provenance, and versioned response-policy documentation.[cite:1][cite:4] This release does not present APPRE as validated artificial intelligence, clinical decision support, a medical device, or a treatment optimizer.[cite:1][cite:4]

Future work may implement APPRE as software, including a rules engine, workflow platform, dashboard, validation layer, provenance system, or AI-assisted research data environment.[cite:1] Any future software implementation will be separate from this documentation release and will require its own intended-use definition, technical validation, privacy and cybersecurity review, user-risk assessment, regulatory analysis, and prospective evaluation before any claim of clinical utility, predictive validity, or decision-support capability is made.[cite:1]

APPRE does not diagnose, prescribe, replace clinician judgment, or optimize controlled-substance exposure in this release.[cite:1][cite:4] Controlled-substance exposure, if present under lawful clinician-governed and ethics-approved research, may be documented only as an as-administered observational field; APPRE does not recommend, adjust, tune, or personalize such exposure.[cite:1][cite:4]

The purpose of this staged approach is to keep the public research record clean and auditable.[cite:1] Documentation architecture, schema stability, provenance, synthetic examples, and reproducibility come first.[cite:1][cite:4] Software, automation, machine learning, and any future productization come later and must be governed as separate implementation layers.[cite:1]

## Boundary summary

- APPRE v0.1.1 is documentation architecture, not clinical software.[cite:1][cite:4]
- Public release includes manuscripts, schemas, synthetic datasets, validation rules, notebooks, and governance files.[cite:1][cite:4]
- Future APPRE software, proprietary workflow systems, and product implementations are separate artifacts unless explicitly licensed otherwise in a later release.[cite:1]
